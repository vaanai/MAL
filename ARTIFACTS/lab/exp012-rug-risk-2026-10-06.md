## Why it was run

On the live probe, one rug (7004b16's C71Lk8Ko, −47,168,631 lamports) cost more than the other 23 fixed-build trades made: they total +1,191,191 lamports (job #195, notebook). The question was whether information known before the migration (holder concentration on the bonding curve, buyer count, the creator's prior mints) can avoid such trades.

## Reading

- **No rule improves the frozen book.** Five of six are negative point estimates. One is significantly negative: `top_wallet_vol_ge_30pct`, whose vetoed trades have a pressure mean of +0.00340, i.e. it removes winners. The nested selection is worse than doing nothing.
- **The rug label is too broad to be a rug label.** Its base rate is 0.5664 of selected entries, because a −30% one-print step is common right after migration. It describes migration volatility, not rugs. A study of real rugs would need a stricter label (for example, the pool's quote side drained in one transaction), and it would spend more tries on these days.
- **Concentrated pools are not worse here.** In the descriptive bins, the most concentrated quintiles (top1_share ≥ 0.43, top3_share ≥ 0.70) have positive pressure means (0.00181, 0.00151). High `n_buyers` (405–1565) has the lowest one-step rate (0.143) and the highest pressure mean (0.00176). That is descriptive, best-of-N, and **not** a rule.
- **Live action: none.** No veto goes to the executor. Rug risk stays part of the strategy's variance, sized by the loss cap.
- Quant-proof is not needed: no edge is claimed, and nothing goes live.

## Can show / cannot show

- Can show: on these 9 days, pre-migration concentration rules at the pre-declared thresholds do not improve SOL per filled trade at the frozen point.
- Cannot show: anything about the holdout, the forward window or live; whether a stricter rug label or other features would work.
