## Why these three

The common cause of P70 to P76 is the taker round trip: about 2.4% of fees (tier `[0,420)` SOL market cap: creator 0.30% + protocol 0.93% + LP 0.02% = 1.25% a leg; tier `[420,1470)`: creator 0.95% + protocol 0.05% + LP 0.20% = 1.20% a leg; `tools/paper_curve_math.py:79-81`) plus a 2-slot exit lag and stop gaps. P78 asks whether a cheap fee tier helps, but the pump fee tiers only reach 0.30% a leg at a market cap of 98,240 SOL, so very few pools will qualify. The creator leg is the biggest piece of the floor at every tier from 420 SOL up (0.95% of the 1.20%). All three proposals ask the same thing from different sides: **can we be on the receiving end of the fees we pay**, using in-protocol routing that exists in the IDL and the official docs, rather than a new signal? None of them needs a signal to be better than the ones that died.

Things I checked and did not propose, with the row that kills or covers them:
- Cheaper venue by fee tier: P78 (PREREG on branch).
- DBC or DAMM v2 cost floor (25 / 10 bps): the tape for them is October only (`dbc-early-momentum`: 3 days) and the DBC start-floor option failed (`p20-dbc-start-floor`, explored-no-survivor). Not testable on Aug/Sep.
- Pump bonding-curve entries and completion lotteries: `lib-a-A16`, `lib-b-B5`, `lib-b-D6`, `lib-c-G1`, `lib-c-I3` dead; P74 and P75 killed (P75 is the closest: pressure-fail -0.197%, CI90 lower bound -0.013387 SOL, stop losses outweigh the enriched completions; a no-stop cell would be "a new cell, not new information", `lib-c-G4.1`).
- Cross-pool atomic arbitrage: `w4-gap-xpool-1` killed at the structure count (0 of 173 sibling pairs with real quote at least 10 SOL); `p20-atomic-xpool-bundle` rests on the same dust siblings.
- Passive LP: P70. Copy trading: P71, `lib-c-Q2.8`. Hour effects: P76. Veto layers on H5: P77, P80 (no independent sample). Mayhem bonding flow: P73 (not priceable); the Mayhem agent flow is P79, not yet pushed, so I do not propose it.
- Creator-as-trader (launching our own coin to collect the creator fee): a wash on our own flow and a platform-integrity problem; dropped.

## 1. CASHBACK-FLOOR: re-cost the frozen H5 trades on cashback coins, where the creator leg is refunded to the trader

**Mechanism.** On a cashback coin the creator fee "is routed to the buyer": each user "would get the creator fee on their swap volume as cashback rather than paying that fee to the coin creator" (`PUMP_CASHBACK_README.md`; accrues in the `UserVolumeAccumulator` PDA, claimed with `claim_cashback`; `Pool` and `BondingCurve` carry an `is_cashback_coin` flag). The docs say new cashback coins stopped on 2026-09-12 (`create_v2` rejects `is_cashback_enabled = [true]`; commit 2026-09-12T14:28:42Z), and existing ones "trade exactly as described", so cashback coins created from 2026-02-17 to 2026-09-12 sit inside the whole Aug/Sep tape. For such a coin the net fee a leg is protocol + LP only: 0.95% instead of 1.25% below 420 SOL market cap (saves 0.30% a leg), and 0.25% instead of 1.20% at 420 to 1,470 SOL (saves 0.95% a leg). H5 buys in the `[0,420)` tier and sells at about 330 s, where the pool is often above 420 SOL after BOOST, so the refund on a round trip is between about 0.60% and 1.25% of stake. That is a lower cost floor by venue and tier, with no new signal.

**Data needed (counts only).** Confirmed present: the frozen H5 set of 1,702 discovery plus 1,124 confirmation trades (2,826, P77 counts table), each with a `pool` address in the clean-view tape rows (`pool` key seen in `/data2/mal/clean-view/fresh-0903/w1/trades`). The tape has no cashback flag (creates columns: mint, creator, trader, name, symbol, is_mayhem_mode, reserves; no `is_cashback`), so Step 0 is one `getMultipleAccounts` pass over the distinct H5 pools reading the `Pool` account byte `is_cashback_coin` (it follows `is_mayhem_mode` at offset 243, `tools/test_pumpswap_mayhem_recipient.py:20`), about 20 calls of 100 pools. Report the cashback share s of pools and of trades per day. Pool accounts are not closed after migration. Not confirmed: that s is large enough. That is the whole point of Step 0.

**The one rule to pre-register.** Take the frozen H5 binding book exactly as P55 priced it (2,826 trades, its reproduction check of max difference 0.0 lamports stays the gate for the code). Change only the cost model: for trades whose pool has `is_cashback_coin` true, add back the creator-leg amount of each leg at the tier of that leg's pre-trade market cap (creator_ppm column of `PUMPSWAP_SOL_FEE_SPLIT`); no other change, no new threshold. Report the repriced split (cashback vs not) under the flat 15% and pressure-fail models, with the gate columns (ex-top-3, ex-best-day, positive days, CI90 lower bound).

**Kill line.** Stop at Step 0 and record NOT_DECIDABLE if cashback trades are below 100 of the 2,826 (no power; the gate needs 100 out-of-sample trades anyway). KILL if, among cashback trades, the repriced mean is at or below 0 under either fail model or the total without the top 3 is at or below 0. Also a KILL for the lane (not for H5) if the non-cashback trades are not worse than the cashback trades before the refund, because then the subset differs in quality and the comparison is not a pure cost effect.

**Capacity.** trades/day x stake x net edge, with the unknown share s: H5 has 2,826 trades over 28 days, about 100 a day. Refund per trade at 0.25 SOL is 0.60% to 1.25%, i.e. 0.0015 to 0.0031 SOL. At s = 10%: 10 trades a day x 0.25 SOL x (0.60% to 1.25%) = 0.015 to 0.031 SOL a day added to whatever H5 earns. This is a small cost lever on one book, not a new book; it only matters if the base H5 mean is within about 1% of zero in the subset. The same refund applies to any PumpSwap rule that trades cashback pools, which P72 (11,812 priced clusters a day) could use as a second population if H5 shows a real effect. Unmeasured: the cashback share, and whether the refund is paid on sells for PumpSwap pools exactly as the docs state (the tx-level `cashback_fee_basis_points` field exists in the events used by `tools/a3_redeploy_check.py:61,141`; a 20-transaction public `getTransaction` check is the second Step 0 item).

**Why it is not dead.** No library row mentions cashback as a cost lever (`grep` over `ideas.jsonl`: only `scheduled-flows-4` CREATORFEE, which notes "cashback closed to new launches", status untested, no test run, and `p20-incentive-farming`, generic and untested). P72 to P76 all priced the full fee. `w2-pump-idl-7` (referral vault, untested) is a different account and a different mechanism. A3 and the H5 fee code treat cashback coins as "not modelled" (`tools/h5_upgrade_dryrun.py:80`), so the lab's H5 numbers on those pools are unproven either way.

## 2. VOLUME-ACCRUAL: is pump's own PUMP-token volume incentive larger than the cheapest atomic round trip?

**Mechanism.** Both pump programs carry a per-user volume incentive in the IDL: `GlobalVolumeAccumulator` {start_time, end_time, seconds_in_a_day, mint, `total_token_supply[30]`, `sol_volumes[30]`}, `UserVolumeAccumulator.current_sol_volume`, and the instructions `init_user_volume_accumulator`, `sync_user_volume_accumulator`, `claim_token_incentives`, and the admin `admin_update_token_incentives` (day number, token supply per day) (`idl_pump.json`, `idl_pump_amm.json`). By the structure of the accounts, a day's reward to a user is the day's token supply times the user's share of that day's SOL volume. If the program is live, the reward per SOL of volume is `total_token_supply[d] x price(PUMP) / sol_volumes[d]`. A buy and sell in one transaction on one pool carries no price exposure and no exit lag (no stop gap), so its cost is two fees plus impact. That is the structural form of "fewer round trips per unit of edge": the edge is a rate paid per unit volume, not a price move. The rebate/maker economics in the sprint criteria, in-protocol.

**Data needed (counts only).** Two account reads, no tape: the `global_volume_accumulator` PDA of the pump program and of the pump AMM program (seed per the IDL), each decoded for `start_time`, `end_time`, `sol_volumes[30]` and `total_token_supply[30]`. The 30-day arrays cover the program's own days, so if the program ran across Aug/Sep, the per-day SOL volume of the whole venue is on chain; cross-check it against the clean-view tape's daily PumpSwap SOL volume (counts, from the manifest-listed hours). PUMP price in SOL: the PUMP/USDC pool 2uF4X used by P76 and a SOL/USDC price (P76 documents both; its 150 USD/SOL assumption must be replaced by the same-day value). Confirmed present: IDL and docs copy in `swarm-1013/w2-pump-idl/raw/`. Not confirmed: that the program is live, funded (`total_token_supply` non-zero) or covered Aug/Sep. If every entry is zero the lane ends in one hour.

**The one rule to pre-register.** Compute, per day d where the arrays are non-zero, `rate_d = total_token_supply[d] x PUMP_in_SOL(d) / sol_volumes[d]` (basis points of SOL volume). Compare with `c = 2 x (fee of the cheapest eligible canonical pool tier) + own impact at 1 SOL`, with fees from `PUMPSWAP_SOL_FEE_SPLIT` at the deepest pool that has a WSOL quote and at least 20 prints an hour (P78 chooses that pool band; use P78's cost-map floor, not a number invented here). One parameter set: stake 1 SOL, one atomic round trip.

**Kill line.** KILL if on no day `rate_d - c` is above 0, or if the median day gives under 5 bps of net, or if the arrays are all zero (program not live in the window: NOT_APPLICABLE). The lane makes no trade in the test, and it does not go beyond the account read without the owner: wash-style volume against a platform programme may breach its terms, (a policy question first; `p20-incentive-farming` note: "may be an owner-policy question (terms of service) before a data one").

**Capacity.** Net bps per volume x volume. If `rate - c` = 0.10% and the lab turns over 1,000 SOL a day: 1 SOL a day. The rate is a share, so it falls as our volume grows: reward to us is `S_d x v / (V + v)`, so net per day peaks at a volume set by `S_d` and `V`, which the account read gives directly. I do not know S_d or V; the lane's output is the number.

**Why it is not dead.** No row names `claim_token_incentives`, `GlobalVolumeAccumulator` or `sync_user_volume_accumulator` (checked in `ideas.jsonl` and the repo docs). `p20-incentive-farming` is the generic untested row and says "Data MAL has: none", aimed at DAMM v2 and DBC, and has no Solana programme named; this is a named in-protocol programme on the venue the lab already trades. Honest prior: low, about 10%. A reward that exceeds a 2 x 0.30% to 2 x 1.2% round trip would be unusual, and the whole programme may be off. It earns its place because it costs two account reads and, if positive, it is the only idea here with no exposure to the stop-gap and lag costs that killed P72 to P76.

## 3. HOLDER-CARRY: a multi-day hold of high-turnover holder-rewards coins, paid the creator fee

**Mechanism.** Since 2026-09-12 a coin can be created as a holder rewards coin: the creator fee "is not paid to a creator wallet. It is set aside for the people who hold the coin, and pump.fun distributes it to them on an ongoing basis"; holders call nothing (`HOLDER_REWARDS_README.md`; `scheduled-flows-4` adds a $20 minimum held, single secondary source). The creator fee is 0.95% of volume from 420 SOL market cap (0.30% below). For a coin with daily volume N times its market cap, the pool of fees is about `0.0095 x N` of market cap a day shared by holders. At N = 3 that is 2.85% a day. This is a carry, so one round trip (2.4% of floor, or about 1.4% for a holder in the 420+ tier on exit, from the same table) is spread over days of holding: fewer round trips per unit of edge. It is the maker-side payout in the sprint criteria, in-protocol.

**Data needed (counts only).** Weaker than the other two. The tape has no holder-rewards flag; the creates rows carry `is_mayhem_mode` only. The Sep 12 to Sep 25 hours exist in the manifest (776 hourly files from 2026-08-14T12 to 2026-09-25T06, `audit-1008/tape/manifest.json`), about 13 days of coins that could be holder-rewards coins. Step 0: read the `BondingCurve` account flag (or the `create_v2` instruction argument 8) for a count of coins created 09-12 to 09-25 by flag, via `getMultipleAccounts` on their bonding-curve PDAs (about 100 per call); report the count per day and the daily PumpSwap SOL volume over market cap for the flagged pools from the tape (counts and ratios only). Not confirmed: that pump.fun's discretionary payout is on a schedule, that a $20 minimum applies, or that the payout reaches wallets that bought on the pool (the README says holders need to do nothing, so on-chain transfers from a pump.fun wallet to holders should be visible in `getSignaturesForAddress` for a sample holder; that is the second Step 0 item).

**The one rule to pre-register.** For flagged pools with 24 h turnover (volume / market cap, both from the tape) of at least 3 on the prior day and at least 24 h of age: buy at 00:00Z (+2 slots), hold 24 h, exit (+2 slots); stake 1 SOL. Net = priced round trip with the real tier fees and the drift from the tape, **plus** the carry credited only as the fee amount implied by the day's tape volume, creator_ppm at the tier, divided by supply and credited only if the Step 0 payout check found a payout to the sampled holders; the carry is not assumed otherwise. One set, no grid.

**Kill line.** KILL if the mean of (drift minus round-trip cost) without the carry is below -(carry upper bound) on the median pool-day, or if fewer than 5 distinct days have flagged pools meeting the turnover test, or if the sampled holders show no payout. P70 measured a mean 24 h price drift of -12.4% (0.25 SOL: -0.031126 SOL on a 0.25 stake) for the top-10-volume pools, which is far above the 2.85% carry, so the lane is only worth running if the flagged subset's drift differs; the first output is that drift alone.

**Capacity.** Pools meeting turnover at least 3 and age at least 24 h: unknown, probably few a day. If 3 pool-days a day x 1 SOL x 1% net = 0.03 SOL a day. Small and highly uncertain. Prior about 5%. It is third because the payout mechanics are off-chain and discretionary, and the data window is 13 days.

**Why it is not dead.** `web-crosschain-6` and `web-forced-flows-9` are other chains' creator coins with no buyer; `hunt23-w3-burn-1` is buy-and-burn (CENSUS-PLAN-2 FAIL); `lib-c-L4`/`p60-feeshare-commit-tag` use the creator-fee split as a commitment signal, not as holder yield; `p64-creator-claim-veto` is parked on claims. No row treats holder rewards as a carry. `hunt23-a-slow-A` (aged survivors, -12% to -24%) is a price-drift test with no carry and no turnover filter.

## Order, and what I would run first

1. CASHBACK-FLOOR (prior of a PASS, mine: about 10% that the subset is large enough and positive; a cost-floor measurement even when it fails). One RPC pass, then an existing priced set re-costed.
2. VOLUME-ACCRUAL (about 10% that the programme is live and pays more than a round trip; two account reads, then stop).
3. HOLDER-CARRY (about 5%; run only if 1 shows that rebate-class routing is paid as documented).

All three need an on-chain read the lab has not made (a flag the tape lacks). That is a fair cost: the tape lacks it because the lab priced every pump trade at the full fee. If Step 0 of all three finds nothing, the next honest conclusion is that the cost floor of this venue is fixed for a taker and the remaining lever is a different signal class with moves well above 4% (fewer, larger bets), not another sub-4% rule.
